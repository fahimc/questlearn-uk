# Learning science and ethical engagement

## Evidence-grounded principles

### Make thinking visible

EEF describes metacognition and self-regulation—planning, monitoring and evaluating learning—as a high-impact, low-cost approach across primary and secondary education. Game missions therefore begin with an objective, offer strategy prompts, and end with a short evidence-based reflection.

### Feedback must change the next action

Correct/incorrect effects are insufficient. Feedback should identify the successful idea or misconception, show a worked or visual model when needed, and give an immediate chance to apply the idea again in a changed context. Digital feedback is not automatically effective; an EEF trial in primary maths found no outcome impact for one digital-feedback programme, reinforcing that product mechanics need validation rather than assumed benefit.

### Retrieval plus application

Short route locks support fluent recall. Building and narrative tasks then require transfer: choose measurements, justify evidence, debug a sequence or explain a design. Mastery evidence requires several successful demonstrations over time and at least one changed context.

### Structured collaboration

EEF reports positive average effects for collaborative learning, but digitally supported collaboration is not inherently better and effective collaboration needs structure. For ages 7–10, start with same-device turn-taking, paired roles and curated signals rather than open chat.

## “Modern game feel” translated ethically

Keep:

- immediate, legible movement and satisfying cause-and-effect;
- short missions inside a larger world;
- creation, collection and discovery;
- visible goals and voluntary challenge routes;
- meaningful cosmetic expression earned through learning/application;
- near-miss feedback that explains a recoverable next step.

Reject:

- variable-ratio paid rewards, loot boxes and artificial scarcity;
- public child leaderboards and shame-based comparison;
- streak loss, energy timers and repeated notifications that punish absence;
- infinite sessions without natural stopping points;
- difficulty changes hidden from learners and educators;
- rewards for disclosure of personal data or social pressure.

## Testable learning hypotheses

| Hypothesis | Measure | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Applied build briefs improve transfer more than decontextualised quiz alone | Near/far transfer item after 7 days | Equal curriculum exposure and time |
| Worked feedback reduces repeated misconception | Same misconception rate over next 3 variants | Do not over-prompt or remove productive struggle |
| Learner route choice increases mission completion | Voluntary completion and child interview | No false choice; all routes reach same objective |
| Session recap supports metacognitive accuracy | Confidence vs later performance calibration | Avoid public or evaluative language |

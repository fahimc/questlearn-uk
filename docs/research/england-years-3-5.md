# England curriculum map: ages 7–10

## Scope and interpretation

England’s Key Stage 2 covers ages 7–11 (Years 3–6). English, mathematics and science are core subjects. Art and design, computing, design and technology, geography, history, languages, music and PE are foundation subjects; maintained schools must also teach religious education and primary relationships and health education.

The statutory requirement is to cover the programme by the end of the key stage. Year-by-year placement is explicit in mathematics and much of English/science, but schools can sequence content differently. The product must therefore let teachers override suggested year placement.

## Year 3 (typical age 7–8)

### Mathematics

- Read, write, order and compare numbers to 1,000; recognise hundreds, tens and ones.
- Add and subtract mentally and with formal column methods up to three digits; estimate and inverse-check.
- Recall and use 3, 4 and 8 multiplication tables; solve multiplication and division problems.
- Count in tenths; recognise, find, write and compare simple fractions; add/subtract fractions with the same denominator within one whole.
- Measure, compare, add and subtract length, mass and volume; work with money; tell time to the minute; use Roman numerals I–XII.
- Measure perimeter of simple 2D shapes; recognise angles, perpendicular/parallel lines and properties of 2D/3D shapes.
- Interpret and present data using bar charts, pictograms and tables.

### English

- Move from decoding towards fluent, independent reading; read fiction, poetry, plays and non-fiction widely.
- Check meaning, ask questions, infer from actions and language, predict from evidence, identify main ideas and retrieve information.
- Plan, draft, evaluate and edit narrative and non-fiction; organise paragraphs around a theme.
- Expand vocabulary; use conjunctions, adverbs and prepositions for time/cause/place; use present perfect; punctuate direct speech.
- Spell prefixes/suffixes, homophones and Year 3–4 statutory words; develop joined handwriting.

### Science

- Work scientifically: questions, fair/comparative tests, careful observation, standard measures, classification, tables/charts and conclusions.
- Plants: functions of parts, requirements for growth, water transport and life cycles.
- Animals including humans: nutrition, skeletons and muscles.
- Rocks: compare properties, fossil formation and soil.
- Light: seeing, reflection, shadows and sun safety.
- Forces and magnets: contact forces, surfaces, poles and attraction/repulsion.

## Year 4 (typical age 8–9)

### Mathematics

- Read, write, order and compare numbers beyond 1,000; round to nearest 10, 100 and 1,000; understand negative numbers in context and Roman numerals to 100.
- Add/subtract four-digit numbers; estimate and inverse-check.
- Recall all multiplication tables to 12 × 12; multiply two/three digits by one digit; identify factor pairs.
- Understand hundredths; recognise equivalent fractions; add/subtract same-denominator fractions; divide one/two-digit numbers by 10 and 100; round and compare decimals to two places.
- Convert units; calculate perimeter and rectilinear area; read/convert analogue and digital time.
- Classify quadrilaterals/triangles, identify acute/obtuse angles, lines of symmetry and coordinates in the first quadrant.
- Interpret and present discrete/continuous data, including bar charts and time graphs.

### English

The lower-Key-Stage-2 English programme spans Years 3–4, with increasing independence by the end of Year 4. Learners should become confident at inference, justification, summarising and discussion; use varied sentence structures and cohesive paragraphing; and edit grammar, punctuation and spelling.

### Science

- Living things and habitats: grouping, classification keys and environmental change.
- Animals including humans: digestive system, teeth and food chains.
- States of matter: solids/liquids/gases, heating/cooling and the water cycle.
- Sound: vibration, medium, pitch, volume and distance.
- Electricity: appliances, simple series circuits, switches, conductors and insulators.

## Year 5 (typical age 9–10)

### Mathematics

- Read, write, order and compare numbers to at least 1,000,000; powers of 10, rounding, negative numbers and Roman numerals to 1,000.
- Add/subtract whole numbers over four digits; multi-step problems; factors, multiples, primes, squares and cubes.
- Multiply up to four digits by one/two digits; divide up to four digits by one digit; use scaling and combinations.
- Compare/order fractions; identify equivalent fractions; mixed and improper fractions; add/subtract denominators that are multiples; multiply proper fractions/mixed numbers by whole numbers.
- Read/write decimals to three places; relate fractions, decimals and percentages; solve percentage and decimal problems.
- Convert metric units and approximate imperial equivalents; area, perimeter and volume; solve time conversions.
- Angles in degrees, properties of shapes, reflection/translation and coordinates.
- Complete, read and interpret tables, including timetables.

### English

- Read a wider range fluently, discuss themes/conventions, make evidence-backed inferences, predict, summarise, distinguish fact/opinion and evaluate authorial language.
- Plan for audience and purpose; develop characters/settings; use organisational devices; assess and edit effectiveness and consistency.
- Use relative clauses, modal verbs/adverbs, cohesion and accurate punctuation; spell the Year 5–6 word patterns and vocabulary.

### Science

- Working scientifically expands to controlling variables, taking repeat measurements, presenting data and using evidence to support/refute ideas.
- Living things: life cycles and reproduction in some plants/animals.
- Animals including humans: changes across the human lifespan.
- Properties and changes of materials: comparison, dissolving, separating mixtures and reversible/irreversible changes.
- Earth and space: solar system, spherical bodies, rotation, day/night and apparent movement.
- Forces: gravity, air/water resistance, friction and mechanisms.

## Foundation subjects: high-value game mappings

### Computing

Design, write and debug programs; use sequence, selection, repetition, variables and input/output; reason about algorithms; understand networks and the web; search and judge content; create digital systems/content; use technology safely and responsibly.

### Geography

Locate countries and major features in the UK, Europe and the Americas; compare places; learn climate zones, biomes, rivers, mountains, volcanoes, earthquakes, settlement, land use, trade, resources, maps, compass points, grid references and fieldwork.

### History

Build chronological knowledge and use historical terms; connect, contrast and identify trends; frame questions; use sources and understand how interpretations differ. Required breadth includes changes in Britain from Stone Age to Iron Age, Roman Britain, Anglo-Saxons and Scots, Vikings, local history, a post-1066 theme, an ancient civilisation depth study, Ancient Greece and a non-European society.

### Design and technology

Research needs, develop criteria, model ideas, make accurately, evaluate and improve. Apply structures, mechanical/electrical systems and computing. Cooking covers healthy diets, savoury dishes and food provenance/seasonality.

### Languages, art, music and PE

Language learning emphasises listening, speaking, reading, writing, vocabulary and basic grammar. Art develops creative work, techniques, evaluation and knowledge of makers. Music combines performance, composition, listening and notation. PE develops broad physical skills, competition, cooperation, evaluation and swimming competence.

## Assessment implications

- Do not infer mastery from one correct answer or completion of a level.
- Capture method and support where possible: attempts, representation, hint use, explanation and transfer to a changed context.
- Distinguish fluency, conceptual understanding and reasoning.
- Teachers remain responsible for curriculum sequencing and formal assessment.

# Devolved-nation curriculum mapping

## Shared product model

Use a jurisdiction-neutral skill graph (for example, `number.multiply.equal-groups`) linked to one or more curriculum references. Never display an English year-level judgement as if it applied across the UK.

| Nation | Typical location for ages 7–10 | Organising model | Product implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| England | Years 3–5, Key Stage 2 | Statutory subject programmes; some year-specific content | Store year and Key Stage references; allow school resequencing |
| Wales | Approx. Progression Steps 2–3 | Continuum with six Areas of Learning and Experience; steps broadly correspond to ages 8 and 11 | Select by demonstrated progression, not age; support Welsh and English |
| Scotland | P4–P6; First moving into Second Level | Experiences and Outcomes plus Benchmarks; First usually to P4, Second usually P5–P7 | One learner may span levels; report Experiences/Outcomes and benchmark evidence |
| Northern Ireland | P4–P7 across Key Stages 1–2 | Areas of Learning, cross-curricular skills and Thinking Skills/Personal Capabilities | Map integrated “World Around Us” contexts and cross-curricular communication/ICT/numeracy |

## Wales

Curriculum for Wales has four purposes and six Areas of Learning and Experience: expressive arts; health and well-being; humanities; languages, literacy and communication; mathematics and numeracy; and science and technology. Literacy, numeracy and digital competence are mandatory cross-curricular skills.

Progression steps are reference points, not age-bound assessment criteria. They broadly correspond to ages 5, 8, 11, 14 and 16. For this audience, content will commonly bridge Steps 2 and 3. The engine should use “I can” descriptions as curriculum links while selecting difficulty from learner evidence.

Product consequences:

- first-class Welsh/English localisation, including audio and text expansion;
- integrated missions align well with Areas rather than isolated subjects;
- represent the five connected mathematical proficiencies: conceptual understanding, communication using symbols, fluency, logical reasoning and strategic competence;
- embed local, national and international contexts and the cross-curricular frameworks.

Sources: [Curriculum introduction](https://hwb.gov.wales/curriculum-for-wales/introduction-to-curriculum-for-wales-guidance), [Literacy and Numeracy Framework](https://hwb.gov.wales/curriculum-for-wales/cross-curricular-skills-frameworks/literacy-and-numeracy-framework-lnf), [Mathematics principles of progression](https://hwb.gov.wales/curriculum-for-wales/mathematics-and-numeracy/principles-of-progression/).

## Scotland

Curriculum for Excellence has eight curriculum areas and four capacities: successful learners, confident individuals, responsible citizens and effective contributors. First Level generally covers P2–P4 and Second Level P5–P7, but achievement is based on evidence, not a hard age gate.

Product consequences:

- model both the Experience and Outcome code and relevant benchmark statements;
- allow a P4 learner to have mixed First/Second Level evidence by skill;
- use interdisciplinary worlds while ensuring discrete literacy, numeracy and health/well-being responsibilities remain visible;
- evidence should be “breadth, challenge and application”, not repeated item accuracy alone.

Sources: [Curriculum levels](https://education.gov.scot/parentzone/curriculum-in-scotland/curriculum-levels/), [First Level benchmarks](https://education.gov.scot/media/1qyaz4tg/firstlevelbenchmarksallareaspdf.pdf).

## Northern Ireland

Primary curriculum stages are Foundation (P1–P2), Key Stage 1 (P3–P4) and Key Stage 2 (P5–P7). Ages 7–10 therefore cross KS1 and KS2. Areas are language and literacy, mathematics and numeracy, the arts, the world around us, personal development and mutual understanding, and PE, with religious education also compulsory.

Product consequences:

- use integrated quests for “The World Around Us” rather than forcing separate history/geography/science labels;
- tag communication, using mathematics and using ICT across missions;
- explicitly represent Thinking Skills and Personal Capabilities such as managing information, problem solving, creativity, working with others and self-management.

Source: [Department of Education statutory curriculum](https://www.education-ni.gov.uk/articles/statutory-curriculum).

## Equivalence warning

Mappings are semantic crosswalks, not claims that the curricula are identical. Each content pack needs educator review in its nation, language and school context before classroom use.
